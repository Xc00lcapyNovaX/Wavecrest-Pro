About Wavecrest Pro

We built the tool
we kept wishing existed.

Wavecrest started with a frustration: hours spent watching successful creators, still unable to articulate why they were winning. We built the tool that reads 200 videos at once and tells you in 90 seconds.

I kept studying the same creators over and over — watching videos, reading comments, checking upload schedules. I could feel there was a pattern. I just couldn't see it.

The problem isn't attention. It's bandwidth. A single person can hold maybe 5–10 videos in working memory at once. A creator's playbook is built across hundreds of them — the hook that gets tested and doubled down on, the thumbnail formula that emerged after 30 flops, the posting cadence optimized through months of trial. You can't see any of that by watching.

So I built Wavecrest. Paste a YouTube channel URL, and it reads up to 200 videos at once — scanning for hook patterns, thumbnail formulas, posting cadence, audience signals, content pillars, monetization angles, and competitive gaps. The full playbook surfaces in 90 seconds.

It's for creators who want to understand what's actually working in their niche, and for brands and agencies that need to decode competitors fast. YouTube first — TikTok and Instagram are next.

How we think

Principles

01

Patterns over impressions

Watching a creator once gives you an impression. Reading 200 videos gives you a pattern. The whole product is built around the latter — because impressions mislead, patterns don't.

02

90 seconds, not 5 hours

The best tools collapse the time between question and answer. Creator research used to take a workday. If Wavecrest isn't delivering that in under two minutes, we've failed at the core problem.

03

No subscription pressure

Creator research isn't a daily need for everyone. The free tier lets you try it once for real. Credits give you more when you need them — on your terms, not a billing cycle that charges you for months you didn't use.

04

Ship first, scale second

We move fast, we're honest about what's not built yet, and we'd rather talk to users than build in assumptions. The changelog is real — we update it when things ship.
